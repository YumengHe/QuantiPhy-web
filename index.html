Reconstructing measurable physical scenes from video

Given a video, reconstruct the scene at real-world scale: what the objects are, how large they are, where they are and how they move. Quantitative questions about size, speed and distance are then measured directly in the reconstructed scene.

Keywords: Minimal Sufficient Reconstruction

Pipeline

Each question is reconstructed on its own from the video, the question and the prior, the known facts that come with it (an object's size or speed, or its distance to the camera). A vision-language model writes the scene as a program: which objects exist, what supports what, and which physical law governs each stretch of motion. Measurement sets the magnitudes: Physics-guided Fitting fits the program to the object masks of all frames under the prior and gravity, and marks every parameter, the metric scale and the asked quantity as determined or undetermined. A checking loop revises the program's structure where the fit shows it is wrong, and keeps an edit only when the evidence it cites improves, nothing that held is lost, and any parameters it adds pay for themselves. Each question then receives two answers: the scene answer, read strictly from the fitted program and left empty when the scene does not determine the asked quantity, and the final answer, always given by a VLM that sees the rendered program and its fitted parameters.

Input and perception→ Scene program→ Physics-guided Fitting→ Checking loop→ Answers

Quantitative Comparison

S / D: the given prior is a size (static) or a velocity / acceleration (dynamic). 2 / 3: 2D, no depth given, or 3D, with object–camera distances given.
